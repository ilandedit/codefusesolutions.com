# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary: owners and decision-makers at businesses with complex or niche software problems, evaluating whether CodeFuse Solutions can be trusted with work other shops turn away. They arrive with a hard problem, not a shopping list.

Secondary (confirmed): prospective employers/recruiters and developer peers. When audience framings conflict, the client audience wins — the site reads as a services company, not a personal portfolio.

## Product Purpose

The company site and portfolio for CodeFuse Solutions. Its job is to present the company beautifully and credibly enough that a business owner reaches out by email. Success is inbound email inquiries; a contact form was considered and deliberately rejected.

## Positioning

CodeFuse Solutions builds custom software for the most complex and niche problems — work that doesn't fit off-the-shelf tools or generalist shops. A templated software product is planned for the future but is explicitly undecided and does not appear on the site yet.

## Operating Context

- v1 is the landing page only: positioning, who CodeFuse is, and a contact link.
- The case-study system is wired underneath (one content file per project, problem → approach → result), but the Work section renders nothing until the first project file exists. Nothing on the live site may look empty or apologetic.
- A commercial flagship project is in development and will become the lead case study when it launches; a few side projects exist but none are ready to publish yet.
- Deployment: static site on GitHub Pages at codefusesolutions.com (domain owned). Repo not yet under git; init + push is part of go-live.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Stack: Astro, fully static output, no backend and no form services.
- Adding a project later must be exactly one content file — no redesign, no new plumbing.
- Contact is a branded address (contact@codefusesolutions.com) forwarded to the founder's inbox; forwarding setup is a go-live task outside the site itself.
- Undecided product facts: the future templated product (nature, name, timing); which side projects will eventually be published.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: CodeFuse Solutions. (Note: Ant Group operates an unrelated AI project named "CodeFuse" — a known naming collision, not a constraint the user has acted on.)
- Solo venture presented as a company: the site speaks as "we", and names Derrick Wengerd as founder (a real human for clients to trust; also serves employer/peer readers).
- Visual identity is being created from scratch — no existing logo, palette, or fonts.
- User-volunteered binding visual direction: light, editorial.
- Standing direction preference (user chose the category standard over conceptual worlds): a conventional, modern studio site executed at the highest craft level — no conceptual carrier, no irony. Craft bar: Work & Co, Instrument, MetaLab.

## Evidence on Hand

None publishable. No shipped case studies, client names, testimonials, metrics, or press. Future work must not fabricate any of these; the Work section stays hidden rather than filled with placeholders.

## Product Principles

- Credibility over volume: fewer, deeper case studies (problem → approach → result) beat a grid of thumbnails; absence beats filler.
- The site itself is quiet proof of craft — it must demonstrate the quality it claims, without saying so.
- Client reading always wins ties; employer and peer value is a by-product, never the frame.
- Every claim on the site must be true today; aspiration (the templated product, future scale) stays off the page until real.
